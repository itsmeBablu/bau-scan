# House radiator close-up survey

During a House walkthrough, radiator detection runs passively with no radiator picker, confirmation panel, corner marking or per-radiator Save button. Three stable geometric observations select a possible body automatically; a closer pass supplies surface samples. At least 120 samples and three viewpoints are required before collecting it automatically. Qualified pending observations are flushed before Finish House. Moving away from an insufficiently observed target releases it so detection can continue elsewhere; it can be reacquired if the user returns for a closer pass.

Automatically collected entries are named **Possible radiator**, carry `identification: automaticCandidate`, and retain scan-estimate provenance. This is not a trained semantic classifier: cabinets and TVs can also match. Inspect or confirm equipment type later in House Options → Radiators. Type confirmation does not verify the dimensions. Hidden rear clearance stays unknown and no mounting-gap assumption is applied during automatic capture.

Before RoomPlan starts, House capture explicitly enables supported scene-depth semantics on its shared AR session after camera permission is granted. The passive observer then reads that session without replacing its delegate, running a second session, changing its configuration during capture or pausing the House camera. Cyan outlines show the current candidate; green outlines mark retained observations, with a live count. There are no live pick/confirm buttons. A missing-depth status is shown rather than pretending capture succeeded. Actual availability of RoomPlan camera depth and radiator recognition rates must be verified on device. If RoomPlan does not expose depth, use a saved-House close-up session instead.

Observations from this uninterrupted session retain House coordinates and are associated with the final room/wall. The saved House 3D/AR viewer includes a radiator envelope when Objects is enabled. Unknown thickness is shown as a flat surface rather than an invented depth. Room/wall associations beyond one metre are explicitly marked approximate. Independent saved-House close-up scans retain their separate coordinate frame and are not falsely placed in the House model. These envelopes are app viewer geometry; Apple's original RoomPlan USDZ remains unchanged.

Open a saved House, choose Options → Radiators, select the room and optional wall reference, then Scan radiator on a LiDAR-equipped iPhone/iPad. This is a separate AR session; continuous House capture and Technical Room capture are unchanged.

The separate saved-House close-up tool retains an explicit corner-free proposal and confirmation. Exclude valves, pipes and brackets from the highlighted body; reject the candidate if the outline is wrong. Move slowly around visible front, side and top surfaces. Finish and review before saving.

Width/height come from the visible front envelope and can refine after further stable, fully visible observations. Wall-to-front distance comes from a parallel adjacent surface proposal, which must be reviewed as wall evidence. Body depth requires a rear-facing surface supported by at least 20 samples from two viewpoints. Wall Abstand means finished wall to the radiator body's back; it is wall-to-front minus observed body depth. Hidden rear surfaces remain unknown. Reflective surfaces, depth noise, incorrect segmentation or wall identification and occlusion affect these estimates; no millimetre accuracy is certified.

There is no generic German/DIN gap applied by default. The optional 30/50 mm examples are explicitly assumed settings for KERMI's long wall console, not universal mounting requirements. Check the actual radiator and bracket instructions: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zxw3laHBoAs . A supported rear observation takes priority. Derived depth also remains labelled as assumed when a mounting assumption supplies the gap.

Entered tape, laser or manufacturer dimensions retain that source; manufacturer values require a reference. Original observations remain available. All numeric survey dimensions and JSON exports use metres. The preview is a survey envelope or observed surface samples, not a detailed engineering model or a registered placement in the House coordinate frame.

The optional `radiators.json` sidecar travels with the `.housescan` package. House rename/recalculation preserves it; duplicate import remaps its House ID. Existing Houses without the sidecar still open normally. Capture requires device validation; simulator tests validate geometry and storage, not physical measurement accuracy.


Detection runs at most once every 1.2 seconds off the main actor, with one in-flight task. A candidate requires three consistent observations, a bounded vertical front and adjacent parallel surface support. Image-border-clipped fronts and missing wall evidence are rejected. Smoothed depth is preferred for proposal normals; no downloaded model, cloud AI or Google sign-in is used. Repeated ribs, column radiators, reflective finishes, occlusion and sparse depth can prevent detection. Width/height are visible-envelope estimates, not guaranteed complete body dimensions. Surface samples, photos and House totals remain bounded by the existing package budgets.

Validation includes synthetic positive/negative geometry tests and source-method provenance. Physical-device validation is still pending; no automatic type-recognition accuracy or dimensional tolerance has been established.
